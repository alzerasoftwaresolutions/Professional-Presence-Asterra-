# Package 3 — Professional Presence
## Example Client — Asterra Manufacturing Group
### About / Corporate Story — Detailed Page Specification

**Version:** 1.0  
**Template:** 01 — Corporate Authority  
**Client:** Asterra Manufacturing Group (fictional example)  
**Status:** Design specification

> This document converts the approved Asterra client-specific design manual and homepage specification into a page-level blueprint for the About / Corporate Story page. It is implementation-ready guidance, not final production copy or code.

---

# 1. Page Purpose

The About page is the primary **corporate identity, history, and organizational context page**.

Where the homepage answers:

> **Who is Asterra, what does the group do, and why is it credible?**

the About page should answer:

> **Where did Asterra come from, what does the organization stand for, how is it structured, and what principles guide its growth?**

The page should move the visitor through:

**Orient → Understand → Discover the Story → Understand the Principles → Meet the Organization → Verify → Engage**

The page should provide greater depth than the homepage without becoming a generic company-profile document.

### Core principle

The About page should make Asterra feel like a **real organization with history, structure, people, and direction**, rather than simply a collection of business units.

---

# 2. Page Role Within the Site

The About page sits immediately after the homepage in the primary corporate narrative.

```text
Homepage
   ↓
Who is Asterra?
   ↓
About / Corporate Story
   ↓
Why does Asterra exist?
How did it develop?
What does it believe?
Who leads it?
How is it organized?
   ↓
Business Units / Capabilities
```

The page should therefore provide the context required to understand the deeper business pages.

### It should not duplicate:

- The full Business Units page
- The complete Projects system
- Full executive biographies
- The entire Resources center
- Detailed contact information
- A long corporate-history archive

Those belong to their respective page families.

# 5. Visual Direction

The page must inherit the approved Asterra identity.

### Core visual language

- Deep evergreen as the primary authority color
- Mineral teal as the accent
- Warm ivory and white as dominant surfaces
- IBM Plex Sans for interface and body text
- IBM Plex Serif selectively for major editorial statements
- Documentary industrial and organizational photography
- Strong horizontal alignment
- Structured 12-column desktop grid
- Editorial asymmetry
- Mostly flat surfaces
- Minimal floating cards
- Restrained motion

The design manual defines Asterra as **industrial, established, precise, confident, responsible, and forward-looking**.

### Distinctiveness rule

Do not introduce:

- Stonebridge navy/copper treatment
- Construction-oriented imagery
- Blueprint motifs
- Orange/copper CTAs
- Generic corporate-office stock imagery
- Consumer-style rounded cards
- Generic SaaS layouts

Asterra's Corporate Authority character should come from **typography, evidence, history, people, industrial imagery, and disciplined composition**.

The About page establishes credibility through **organizational history and development**.

```text
Milestone / Period
Story / development

Story / development
```

```text

YEAR / PERIOD        STORY
```

# 11. History Timeline Rules

The timeline should communicate **progression**, not merely dates.

Each milestone should answer at least one of:

- What changed?
- What capability was established?
- What market was entered?
- What organizational development occurred?
- What strategic direction emerged?

### Milestone anatomy

```text
PERIOD / YEAR

Milestone title

Short description explaining the significance
of the development.

Optional supporting image
```

### Content rule

Only approved historical facts should be used.

Because Asterra is a fictional example client, all dates, acquisitions, facilities, markets, founders, executives, and achievements remain placeholders until intentionally supplied.

Never invent a corporate history merely to make the timeline visually complete.

# 20. Responsive Composition

## Desktop — >= 1100px

- 12-column conceptual grid
- Large editorial hero
- Asymmetric company overview
- Full timeline treatment
- Large mission/vision typography
- Horizontal or grid-based leadership
- Structured business-group composition
- Restrained recognition row
- Full-width CTA

## Tablet — 768px–1099px

- Reduce complex layouts to two-column structures
- Reduce display typography
- Simplify timeline composition
- Maintain editorial hierarchy
- Allow leadership to move toward 2-column presentation
- Reduce image dominance where necessary

## Mobile — < 768px

```text
Header
↓
Hero
↓
Company Overview
↓
Story
↓
Timeline
↓
Mission / Vision / Values
↓
How We Operate
↓
Leadership
↓
Business Structure
↓
Recognition
↓
CTA
↓
Footer
```

### Mobile rules

- Hero becomes vertical
- Large statements remain visually dominant
- Timeline becomes vertical
- Mission and Vision stack naturally
- Values become a readable list rather than tiny cards
- Leadership becomes a vertical list/grid
- Business structure becomes editorial list
- Recognition wraps naturally
- CTA remains prominent

### Mobile principle

> **Recompose the corporate story; do not shrink the desktop composition.**

This follows the established Asterra responsive rule.

# 24. Motion

The About page should use motion primarily to support **narrative progression**.

### Recommended

- 250–400ms interface transitions
- 400–650ms content reveals
- Subtle image scale on hover
- Timeline reveal
- Small header transitions
- Controlled image reveal

### Avoid

- Constant parallax
- Scroll-jacking
- Large looping animations
- Animated statistics without purpose
- Decorative motion behind body text

### Reduced motion

When `prefers-reduced-motion` is enabled:

- Remove non-essential reveals
- Disable parallax
- Disable continuous movement
- Preserve content visibility
- Keep interaction states functional

The Asterra motion system is defined as **quiet, confident, and industrially precise**.

---

# 25. Accessibility Requirements

The About page must have:

- One clear H1
- Semantic heading hierarchy
- Descriptive link labels
- Visible keyboard focus states
- Sufficient text/background contrast
- Meaningful alt text for informative imagery
- Empty alt attributes for decorative imagery
- Keyboard-accessible timeline/navigation
- Accessible leadership links
- Accessible recognition links
- Accessible CTA controls
- Reduced-motion support

### Timeline accessibility

The visual timeline must not communicate meaning through position alone.

Each milestone must remain understandable in linear reading order.

### Leadership accessibility

Portraits must not be the only method of identifying a person.

Names and roles must be text content.

# 26. Performance Requirements

Priority order:

1. Header
2. Hero typography and image
3. Company overview
4. First story/timeline content
5. Remaining imagery
6. Leadership and recognition media

### Rules

- Use responsive hero image variants
- Reserve image dimensions to prevent layout shifts
- Load only required IBM Plex weights
- Optimize historical and leadership photography
- Defer lower-page images when appropriate
- Avoid unnecessary third-party embeds
- Keep timeline interactions lightweight
- Avoid large JavaScript animation libraries for simple transitions

The page should remain fast even though it contains substantial narrative content.
