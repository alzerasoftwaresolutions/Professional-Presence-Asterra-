# Asterra Leadership / Management Team — Detailed Page Specification

**Package:** Package 3 — Professional Presence
**Template:** Template 01 — Corporate Authority
**Example Client:** Asterra Manufacturing Group
**Page:** Leadership / Management Team — Listing
**Route:** `/leadership`
**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Design & implementation specification

> This specification follows the established Package 3 architecture, Information Architecture & Routing, Universal Design Analysis, Template 01 — Corporate Authority, Asterra Design Manual, and the previously defined Asterra page specifications.

---

# 1. Page Purpose

The Leadership page introduces the people responsible for guiding Asterra.

It should answer:

> **Who leads Asterra, and what kind of organization is behind the business?**

The page should strengthen organizational credibility without turning the website into a conventional corporate employee directory.

The emphasis is:

**People → Responsibility → Expertise → Organizational Confidence**

---

# 2. Page Objectives

The page should:

* Introduce key leadership figures.
* Show organizational responsibility clearly.
* Reinforce trust and institutional credibility.
* Give appropriate context about leadership roles.
* Connect leadership to Asterra's broader corporate story.
* Provide deeper profiles only where useful.
* Maintain the restrained Corporate Authority visual language.

The page should **not** attempt to show every employee.

---

# 5. Primary User Questions

Visitors should be able to understand:

1. Who leads Asterra?
2. What are their responsibilities?
3. What areas of the organization do they oversee?
4. What experience is relevant to their role?

# 9. Section 01 — Leadership Hero

## Objective

Introduce leadership as part of Asterra's organizational identity.

### Eyebrow

**LEADERSHIP**

### H1 direction

> **Leadership grounded in experience and responsibility.**

Alternative:

> **The people guiding Asterra forward.**

The final copy should be based on approved brand messaging.

### Supporting copy

A concise introduction explaining that Asterra is guided by leaders responsible for its businesses, people, operations, and long-term direction.

---

# 10. Hero Composition

Preferred:

```text
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ LEADERSHIP                                                   │
│                                                             │
│ LEADERSHIP GROUNDED IN       Leadership portrait /          │

│ RESPONSIBILITY.                                             │

└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The page should feel institutional and confident.

Avoid making the hero look like a celebrity profile page.

# 11. Section 02 — Leadership Introduction

## Objective

Explain how leadership functions within Asterra.

Recommended structure:

```text
OUR LEADERSHIP

Large editorial statement

Short supporting paragraph
```

Possible message direction:

> Asterra's leadership brings together operational, technical, commercial, and organizational experience across the businesses we serve.

The actual copy must be based on approved content.

---

# 13. Leadership Card Anatomy

Each leadership card should contain:

### Required

* Portrait
* Full name
* Position / role
* Profile link where a detail page exists

### Optional

* Short responsibility statement
* Business unit
* Area of expertise

Avoid displaying:

* Long biographies
* Decorative badges
* Excessive statistics
* Social media icons unless genuinely relevant

---

# 14. Portrait Art Direction

Leadership portraits should establish a consistent visual standard.

Preferred:

* Consistent framing
* Similar background treatment
* Professional but natural expression
* Consistent crop
* Consistent lighting

The portraits should feel like part of one organization.

Avoid mixing:

* Professional studio portraits
* Casual phone photographs
* Heavily edited images
* Different aspect ratios

unless the content constraints require it.

---

# 15. Portrait Treatment

The design should not rely on circular profile avatars.

Preferred:

* Editorial rectangular portrait
* Vertical portrait
* Structured image frame

Example:

```text
┌─────────────────────┐
│                     │
│                     │
│      PORTRAIT       │
│                     │
│                     │
└─────────────────────┘
NAME
POSITION
```

This is more aligned with the Asterra Corporate Authority direction.

---

# 16. Leadership Hierarchy

The visual hierarchy should communicate organizational responsibility.

Recommended:

```text
Executive Leadership
        ↓
Senior Management
        ↓
Functional / Business Leadership
```

Do not make every person visually identical if their organizational responsibility is significantly different.

However, avoid exaggerated visual hierarchy that makes some employees appear unimportant.

# 19. Section 05 — Leadership Principles

## Objective

Connect individual leadership to organizational behavior.

This section should not become a generic "our values" section if those values already belong to the About page.

Instead, it should focus specifically on how leadership operates.

Possible themes:

```text
RESPONSIBILITY
How decisions are made.

EXPERTISE
How technical knowledge informs decisions.

ACCOUNTABILITY
How commitments are managed.

LONG-TERM THINKING
How Asterra approaches sustainable growth.
```

Only use principles that are supported by the actual brand strategy.

---

# 20. Leadership Principles Layout

Preferred:

```text
HOW WE LEAD

01  Responsibility
    Short explanation

02  Expertise
    Short explanation

03  Accountability
    Short explanation
```

Use editorial hierarchy rather than colorful cards.

---

# 21. Section 06 — Organizational Connection

## Objective

Connect leadership to the wider organization.

Possible structure:

```text
LEADERSHIP ACROSS THE BUSINESS

Business Units

[Business Unit]    [Business Unit]    [Business Unit]

[Explore Our Businesses]
```

This creates a useful connection:

```text
Leadership
    ↓
Business Units
    ↓
Capabilities
    ↓
Projects
```

This section should be concise.

---

# 22. Leadership Profile Detail

Where deeper biographical information is appropriate, each leader can link to:

```text
/leadership/[person-slug]
```

The detail page can include:

* Name
* Position
* Portrait
* Biography
* Responsibilities
* Expertise
* Relevant business unit
* Professional background
* Related insights where appropriate

The listing page should remain concise.

---

# 23. When Not to Create Individual Profiles

Do not create a profile detail page simply because a person's name appears on the Leadership page.

A detail page should exist only when there is enough useful, approved information to justify it.

For a small leadership team, the listing page may be sufficient.

---

# 24. Final CTA

The CTA should connect organizational credibility to business engagement.

Recommended:

> **Let's discuss what you're working on.**

Supporting copy:

Connect with the appropriate Asterra team to discuss your requirement.

Actions:

**Contact Asterra**

**Explore Our Businesses**

# 25. Responsive Composition

## Desktop

* Large editorial hero
* Executive portraits in 2–3 columns
* Management section in a structured grid/list
* Leadership principles in editorial columns
* Organizational connection near the bottom

## Tablet

* Two-column leadership portraits
* Management moves toward two columns
* Principles remain readable
* CTA remains full-width

## Mobile

```text
Hero
↓
Introduction
↓
Executive Leadership
↓
Management
↓
Leadership Principles
↓
Business Connection
↓
CTA
```

Leadership cards should become a single-column or carefully structured two-column layout depending on screen width.

Do not shrink portraits excessively to preserve multiple columns.

# 26. Typography

Continue the established Asterra system.

### IBM Plex Sans

Use for:

* Names
* Positions
* Navigation
* Metadata
* Supporting copy
* Buttons

### IBM Plex Serif

Use selectively for:

* Hero headline
* Major editorial statement
* Leadership principle statement
* CTA headline

Names should remain highly readable and prominent.

---

# 27. Color

Maintain the Asterra palette:

* Deep Evergreen
* Mineral Teal
* Warm Ivory
* White
* Ink
* Supporting grays

Recommended:

* Warm Ivory for main editorial sections
* White for profile content
* Deep Evergreen for major structural areas
* Mineral Teal for links and interactions

Do not introduce individual colors for different departments.

---

# 28. Interaction

Recommended:

* Subtle portrait hover
* Arrow/link movement
* Profile transition
* Section reveal

Avoid:

* Animated portraits
* Large hover overlays
* Rotating profiles
* Auto-changing leadership carousels
* Excessive motion

The people should remain the focus.

---

# 29. Accessibility

Required:

* One H1
* Semantic heading structure
* Keyboard-accessible profile links
* Descriptive link labels
* Visible focus states
* Appropriate portrait alt text
* Sufficient contrast
* Reduced-motion support

### Portrait alt text

Use:

> `Portrait of [Name], [Position]`

when the person's identity is important to the content.

---

# 30. Content Integrity

Leadership information is highly sensitive to accuracy.

Never invent:

* Names
* Positions
* Professional history
* Qualifications
* Awards
* Years of experience
* Responsibilities
* Educational credentials
* Professional memberships

All leadership information must come from approved client content.

---

* Leadership listing

Potentially appropriate:

* Organization
* Person
* BreadcrumbList

Use `Person` only for actual published individual profiles.

---

# 35. Performance

Leadership pages should remain lightweight.

Priorities:

* Optimized portraits
* Responsive image sizes
* Lazy loading for below-fold portraits
* Reserved image dimensions
* Limited font weights
* No unnecessary profile animation

Portrait quality matters, but oversized source images should never be loaded unnecessarily.

# 36. AI Implementation Guidance

An AI design or coding agent should:

1. Follow Package 3.
2. Follow Universal Design Analysis.
3. Use Template 01 — Corporate Authority.
4. Follow the Asterra Design Manual.
5. Preserve the established typography.
6. Preserve the established color system.
7. Treat leadership as organizational evidence.
8. Use consistent portrait treatment.
9. Establish clear leadership hierarchy.
10. Avoid generic employee-directory design.
11. Never fabricate biographical information.
12. Use structured leader data.
13. Keep the listing page concise.
14. Create individual profiles only when justified.
15. Connect leaders to relevant business units.
16. Maintain accessibility.
17. Optimize portrait imagery.
18. Keep interactions restrained.
19. Preserve the visual relationship with About, Business, and Projects pages.

# 37. Anti-Patterns

Do not introduce:

* Generic LinkedIn-style employee directory
* Circular avatar grids
* Excessive profile cards
* Fake biographies
* Fake credentials
* Fake awards
* Department color coding
* Carousel-only leadership presentation
* Excessive animations
* Celebrity-style portraits
* Social-media-heavy profiles
* Stonebridge styling
* Separate visual identity from the rest of Asterra
